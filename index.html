Elisa Todarello

Postdoctoral Fellow, University of California, Berkeley
Marie Skłodowska-Curie Fellow, University of Turin

I am a theoretical physicist working at the interface of particle physics, astrophysics and cosmology. My research focuses on axions and other ultralight dark matter candidates: how they behave as dark matter, and how we can detect them with telescopes, laboratory experiments and astrophysical observations.

Before Berkeley, I was a postdoc at the University of Nottingham, the University of Turin and the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology. I received my PhD from the University of Florida in 2018.

Research

Most of the matter in the Universe is invisible: we see its gravitational pull, but we don't know what it is made of. A compelling possibility is the axion, a very light particle originally proposed to solve a puzzle in the theory of the strong nuclear force. If dark matter is made of axions or other ultralight particles, it should leave faint fingerprints all around us: a dim spectral line from decaying axions, X-rays from the Sun, tiny shifts in the orbits of satellites. I work out what these signals would look like, and search for them in astronomical data.

Axion decay across the electromagnetic spectrum

Axion dark matter can decay into two photons, producing a narrow spectral line. I search for this line in astronomical data from radio to X-rays.

Ultraviolet legacy data (JCAP 2026) · Axions shining in the ultraviolet (JCAP 2025) · MUSE-Faint dwarf galaxies (JCAP 2024)

The Sun as an axion laboratory

Axions produced in the solar core can convert into X-rays in the solar magnetic field. With experimental colleagues, I used the NuSTAR X-ray telescope as an axion helioscope.

NuSTAR as an axion helioscope (PRL 2025) · Axion-nucleon and axion-electron couplings (2026) · The Sun as a dark matter target (PLB 2024)

Stimulated decay and the axion echo

A large photon population stimulates axion decay: a powerful beam sent into the dark matter halo returns a faint "echo". I study this effect for laboratory searches and astrophysical sources.

Coherent versus incoherent amplification (2026) · Astrophysical echoes (JCAP 2024) · Axion echo (PRD 2022) · How to suppress exponential growth (JCAP 2020)

Quadratically coupled ultralight dark matter

Dark matter that couples quadratically to ordinary matter is screened or amplified by surrounding matter. I study how this affects searches, from laboratory cavities to satellite orbits around Earth.

LAGEOS II pericentre precession (2026) · Impact of cavities (PRD 2026)

The wave nature of axion dark matter

Axion dark matter is a highly degenerate quantum field. I have studied when it is described by a classical field, its gravitational self-interactions, and the survival of axion miniclusters in the galaxy.

Axion miniclusters (JCAP 2022) · Gravitational self-interactions (PRD 2018) · Duration of classicality (PLB 2017)
